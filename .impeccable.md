# Design Context — Dalton Coding

## Target audience
Mercedes owners in Podgorica, Montenegro. Mostly arriving from Instagram on a
phone, at night, idly. Not enthusiasts on a forum — ordinary owners who do not
know their car already contains the features being sold.

## Use cases
1. Understand that their own car has dozens of dormant factory options.
2. See specifically which ones, and where in the cabin they live.
3. Call to book. Conversion is a phone call, not a form.

## Brand personality
A one-person specialist shop. Black and gold. Credible rather than flashy:
the pitch is "affordable, quality, lifetime warranty", not luxury posturing.
The product IS the car's interior at night — ambient lighting, lit screens.

## Art direction rules for this project
- The photograph is the only place cyan/neon appears. It is the product, not
  decoration. UI chrome must NOT also be cyan, or the whole thing collapses
  into generic neon-on-dark.
- UI chrome is warm: near-black tinted toward brown, brand gold, warm off-white.
  The warm/cool tension against the cabin is the point.
- No glass, no blur panels, no card grids, no gradient text. The earlier
  revision used all of these and the client rejected it outright.
- Annotation, not cards. The reference language is a workshop manual callout:
  hairline leader lines, small-caps letterspaced labels, precise pins.
- The cabin photo must stay readable. UI sits on it, never covers it.

## Typography
Display: Archivo (heavy, tight tracking, wide optical size).
Body: Public Sans. Explicitly NOT Inter.
